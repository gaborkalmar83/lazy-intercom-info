Privacy Policy

Lazy Intercom for Android (package com.kalmarium.lazyintercom). Last updated: 26 November 2026.

The short version
  • There are no accounts. We, Kalmarium Services BV, run no servers for the app and collect no personal data from you.
  • Your voice messages are recorded only while you hold the button, stored on your phone, and sent only to the other phones in your own household, encrypted.
  • The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. Google, not us, collects the data needed for that, after asking for your consent where the law requires it.
  • You can remove everything the app holds by choosing Leave household in Settings or by uninstalling.

1. Who we are

Lazy Intercom is published by Kalmarium Services BV ("Kalmarium", "we", "us"). For privacy questions, to exercise your rights, or for anything else, write to kalmariumservices@gmail.com.

2. What Lazy Intercom does

Lazy Intercom is a household voice intercom. You hold a button, speak, and the recording plays on the other phones in your household, and optionally on speakers in your home. Phones find each other on your home Wi-Fi and join the same household with an invite QR code.

3. Data we collect

None. We do not run any server that receives your data, we do not have user accounts, and the app does not send your messages, contacts, names or settings to us. We cannot see your messages, even if we wanted to.

Some data does exist on your phone or is handled by Google, as explained below.

5. How messages travel and how they are protected

At home

Phones talk directly to each other over your home Wi-Fi. To find each other, the app uses local network discovery: mDNS announcements, a small UDP broadcast, and scanning the addresses of your Wi-Fi network. This is used only to find your household's devices, and it never leaves your local network.

Encryption

Every message sent between household phones, over Wi-Fi or over the optional relay, is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using the household key. Only phones that joined your household hold that key.

Speakers

To play on a speaker, the sending phone briefly serves the audio clip on your local network and tells the speaker (Google Cast, DLNA/UPnP or a Home Assistant media player) to fetch it. This stage is not encrypted by the app because speakers cannot decrypt it, so it stays on your local network. Use it on a network you trust.

Optional away-from-home relay

If you turn it on, you create your own Azure Web PubSub resource in your own Microsoft Azure account and enter its address and key in Settings. Encrypted messages then pass through that resource when phones are not on the same Wi-Fi. The relay only sees encrypted data and cannot read your messages. The resource belongs to you, and Microsoft processes it under your own agreement with them. We have no access to it.

7. Scanning QR codes

To join a household, the app uses Google's code scanner, which is part of Google Play services. Scanning happens on your device. We do not receive the camera image or the code.

8. Ads (Google AdMob)

The free version shows one small banner ad on the main screens (never on the incoming-message card). Ads are served by Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google collects and uses data such as your advertising ID, IP address, device and app information and how you interact with ads, under Google's Privacy Policy. We do not receive that data, other than reports of ad performance in aggregate.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, Google's consent form (the User Messaging Platform) asks for your choice first, and ads that use your data are only requested according to your answer. You can change your choice later in the app's privacy settings, where offered. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings (Settings, Privacy, Ads).

You can remove the banner permanently with the Remove ads purchase.

9. Purchases (Google Play Billing)

The one-time Remove ads purchase (EUR 1.99) is handled by Google Play. Google processes your payment and tells the app whether the purchase exists, so the banner stays off. We do not see your payment details. Google's terms and privacy policy apply to the purchase.

10. Sharing and selling data

We do not sell data and we do not share your data with anyone, because we do not have it. The only third-party services involved are Google (AdMob, Google Play Billing, Google code scanner via Google Play services) and, if you set it up yourself, your own Azure relay and your own Home Assistant.

11. Children

Lazy Intercom is intended for adults. It is not directed at children, and its ads are not designed for children. If you let a young person use a phone that has the app installed, you are responsible for that decision. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that concerns you, contact us and we will help, although we hold no personal data we could delete.

12. How long data is kept, and deleting it

  • Voice messages are deleted from the phone automatically after the retention period you choose (30 days by default).
  • Leave household in Settings removes the household key, the paired devices and the stored messages from that phone.
  • Uninstalling the app removes all of its data from the phone.
  • Data held by Google (ads, purchases) is deleted or managed through your Google account and Google's own tools.

13. Your rights

If you are in the EEA, the UK or a similar jurisdiction, you have rights under the GDPR to access, correct, erase, restrict and port your personal data, to object to processing, and to withdraw consent at any time. Because we do not hold your personal data, there is usually nothing for us to provide or erase, but you can email us at kalmariumservices@gmail.com and we will answer within one month. You can also complain to your national data protection authority.

The legal bases on which data is processed are: your consent (advertising, microphone and other permissions), performance of the service you asked for (sending messages between your own devices), and legitimate interest in keeping the app secure.

14. Diagnostics and problem reports

From version 1.2.2, if the app crashes, the crash text (an error trace with a time stamp) is saved in the app's private storage on your phone, and the app keeps up to the last 200 diagnostic lines of the current session (ad and purchase status messages) in memory only. Settings, Diagnostics shows exactly what a problem report contains: the app name and version, your phone make, model and Android version, your language, the ad and purchase status, the last crash and the last log lines. The app never sends this report itself and uploads nothing. Only if you tap Send report does your own e-mail app open with a draft addressed to kalmariumservices@gmail.com, which you can read, change or discard before sending. If no e-mail app is found, the report is copied to the clipboard instead. There is no crash-reporting service and no third-party diagnostics SDK in the app.

15. Security

Messages are encrypted end to end as described above. The app contains no analytics or tracking software of our own. No system is perfectly secure: if you lose a phone that belongs to your household, use Leave household on the other phones and create a new household, which gives you a new key.

16. Website visits

This website counts page views with a cookie-free script that stores nothing on your device and sends only the page address, the referring website and the country (derived from your network address, which is not kept). It is switched off if your browser sends Do Not Track.

17. AI transparency

The app contains no AI features. The illustrations on our website and in the Google Play listing were generated with an AI image model (Azure gpt-image), and we say so here in line with the EU AI Act's transparency expectations.

18. Changes to this policy
