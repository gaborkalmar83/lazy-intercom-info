Talk to family in another room without shouting

Dinner is ready, and the person you need is two floors up with headphones on. Shouting does not work, and a text message may sit unread. Here are the usual options, what is good and bad about each, and how a WiFi intercom app on the phones you already have fits in.

The usual options

  • Shouting. Free and instant, but it fails with closed doors, multiple floors and noise-cancelling headphones.
  • A phone call. It works when the person answers. It interrupts them, and a phone on silent will not ring through.
  • A chat message. Easy, but it is silent on a locked phone and invisible to someone with a game or a video call on screen.
  • Smart speaker announcements. Handy if you already own compatible speakers in the right rooms. They only reach rooms that have one, and they need the speakers to be set up.
  • Wired or dedicated intercom hardware. Reliable, but it costs money and often needs installing.
  • A WiFi intercom app. It uses the phones everyone already carries, so there is nothing to install on the wall.

How Lazy Intercom works

Lazy Intercom plays your own recorded voice on the other family phones. You hold a button, speak and let go. The other phones wake with a full-screen card that says who the message is from, with Got it, Play again and Reply buttons. A short sound plays first, then a pause, then your voice, through whatever the phone is using, including Bluetooth earbuds.

If nobody taps Got it within 45 seconds, the message replays once, louder. Every message is kept, so it can be played again later, and Delivered and Heard ticks show what arrived. You can also pick one of 10 built-in sound effects and set the pause before the voice, which is 3 seconds by default.

Set it up in about a minute

  1. Install the app on each family phone. There are no accounts and no sign-up.
  2. The first phone creates your household. The other phones scan its invite QR code from Settings.
  3. The phones find each other automatically on your home WiFi.
  4. Hold the button, speak and let go.

Honest limits

  • It is not a live walkie-talkie or a call. Messages are short recorded clips, and people reply when they can.
  • It is Android only, and every phone that should receive messages needs the app. People with an iPhone can still hear you when the message plays on Google Cast, DLNA or Home Assistant speakers.
  • The phones talk directly over your home WiFi. An optional away-from-home relay uses your own free Azure Web PubSub resource, and it does not store messages for phones that are offline.
  • Some phone brands stop background apps to save battery. See why a phone stops getting messages.
  • The free version shows one small ad banner from Google AdMob, never on the incoming-message card. A one-time purchase removes it.

It suits families and flatmates who already carry phones, and who want a voice that cuts through headphones without a wall-mounted device. It is not a replacement for an emergency call.

Questions, answered

Does the other person need the app too?

Yes. Every phone that should receive messages needs Lazy Intercom, and it is Android only. People with an iPhone can still hear you when the message plays on Cast, DLNA or Home Assistant speakers.

Does it work with the screen off and headphones in?

Yes, that is the point. The message wakes the screen with a full-screen card and plays through whatever the phone is using, including Bluetooth earbuds.

Is it a live walkie-talkie or a call?

No. Messages are short recorded clips that play on the other phones, not a live call. Phones that are offline when you send do not receive the message later through the optional relay.
